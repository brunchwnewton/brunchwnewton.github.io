For a century, the measurement problem has been treated as either an unsolvable mystery or a matter of philosophical taste. Aethic reasoning proposes that it is neither: it is a logical problem with a logical solution.

The Ontology of Time — The Extrusion Principle
Foundation · The shape of reality itself
+

Aethic reasoning begins with a radical restructuring of what time is. The block universe (all of spacetime as a static 4D object) is compatible with relativity but can’t account for temporal flow or quantum collapse. The growing block universe captures flow but clashes with relativity. The resolution: reality is not one block universe but a Markov chain of block universes. Each node is a complete, static, relativistic spacetime. An observer’s experience of time is their movement along this chain, driven by the acquisition of information. An additional degree of freedom — the “Aethic dimension” — is orthogonal to spacetime itself.

The driving analogy: imagine driving past a forest. At 2:00 PM you glance left and see a young tree; at 2:05 you see an old tree. Copenhagen says the car is parked and one tree grew up. Aethic reasoning says you moved — you were always looking at two different trees (two different block universes) with two different histories.

The Extrusion Principle When an observer gains information, they do not collapse a wavefunction within their current universe. They extrude — transition to a different block universe whose entire history is consistent with the new knowledge. The particle never changed; your Aethus did.

The prior block universe retains its superposition indefinitely; the subsequent one never had it. Different block universes are not spatially separate — they occupy the same physical space, with differences encoded informationally (the “qualitative facet” of extrusion). This single principle dissolves the retrocausality problem, the EPR locality problem, and the tension between eternalism and quantum dynamics — all at once.

The indiscriminate time principle holds that uncertainty fans out into the past just as it does into the future; the ontological nature of not-knowing is the same in both directions. Consider the Battle of Brémule, fought in the High Middle Ages: unless you already know the outcome, it is in Aethic superposition relative to your Aethus right now — even though it happened 900 years ago. Upon learning the English won, you extrude to a block universe where the English always won.

Zoe’s time-travel password makes the re-rolling precise. Zoe must crack a 5-option password and can jump back 5 minutes. Classically, she uses process of elimination (expected ≈ 3 tries). Aethically, each jump erases her knowledge — she returns to a prior block universe where the correct answer is re-rolled. She expects 5 tries (geometric distribution), not 3. And Jenny’s Japanese extends this to identity: your best friend reveals she secretly learned Japanese at 30; you accidentally time-travel back 3 hours and the secret re-rolls — Jenny learned Italian instead. Rewind further and progressively more of her personality gets randomized, creating a Ship-of-Theseus interpolation rather than the classical all-or-nothing.

Jenny is your childhood best friend. You’ve known each other since you were both two. You know everything about her. That’s what makes it so shocking when, at her 30th birthday party, with the cameras rolling and everyone singing, she starts speaking fluent Japanese with Tim, your brother-in-law from Japan, seconds before she blows out the candles. After the party, she tells you the whole story: she secretly taught herself Japanese starting at age ten. She worked her way from library books to YouTube to online classes. Twenty years, and you had no idea.

Now here’s where it gets strange. Two hours after the party, going to sleep, you accidentally activate a time-travel device your colleague Zoe gave you. It sends you back three hours, to ten minutes before the candles.

According to this framework, something disturbing happens. You rewound to before Jenny told you she speaks Japanese, which means the information hasn’t been transferred to you yet. And because it hasn’t been transferred, it gets re-rolled. When Tim says a few words in Japanese at the candles, Jenny blinks. She doesn’t understand. She never learned Japanese. She never had that secret life. It’s gone.

But here’s the truly unsettling part: Tim also studied Italian in college. When he switches to Italian, this version of Jenny perks right up. Apparently she secretly taught herself Italian instead.

Jenny learned Japanese twenty years ago. You only traveled back three hours. And it still re-rolled. Because what matters isn’t when the fact happened. It’s when you learned it. The framework says you don’t live in one fixed story. You live in a version of reality determined by what information you’ve received. Rewind past the moment you received it, and the story changes, even for events twenty years in the past.

Rewind further, past the age where you learned her favorite song, her weird hobby, her childhood nickname, and each of these re-rolls too. Progressively more of Jenny’s personality gets randomized. Not all at once, like in a movie where the person was never born. Piece by piece, like a Ship of Theseus, until at some point you have to ask: is this even still Jenny?

This is not a metaphor. This is the framework’s first prediction, and it comes from one radical idea:

The particle never changed — you did. When you learn something new, you don’t change the world. You move to a different, complete version of reality that was always consistent with what you just learned. The old version, where it was still uncertain, is still out there. You just aren’t in it anymore.

Here’s the physics this solves. For a hundred years, physicists have known that looking at a tiny particle, like an electron, changes what it does. Before you look, it’s doing multiple things at once. The moment you look, it “collapses” into doing one thing. Nobody knows why. This is called the measurement problem.

Aethic reasoning says: looking doesn’t change the particle. You moved. Think of it like driving past a forest. At 2:00 PM you see a young tree. At 2:05 you see an old tree. The old explanation is like saying the car was parked and the tree magically aged. Aethic reasoning says you drove forward — you were always looking at two different trees.

Each “spot” along the road is a complete version of reality: a full photograph of the universe, past and future included, and when you learn something new, you shift to a different photograph. The one you left behind? Still there. The electron in that version is still doing multiple things at once. It didn’t collapse. You just moved to a version where it was always doing the one thing you saw.

And this works backward in time. Right now, if you don’t know who won the Battle of Brémule in 1119 AD, both outcomes are equally real from your perspective. The moment you learn the English won, you shift to a version where they always won. Before you learned that? Genuinely undecided. Not because history is fuzzy, but because your relationship to that information hadn’t been established yet. Same mechanism as Jenny’s Japanese. Same mechanism as the electron. What you don’t know isn’t just “missing information.” It determines which version of reality you’re living in.

The takeaway: When you observe something, you don’t collapse anything or change anything. You transition to a different, complete version of reality that was always consistent with what you just learned. The version you left behind still exists with its uncertainty intact. This is the Extrusion Principle — the foundation everything else is built on.

Why This Extra Dimension Is Forced

The extrusion principle doesn’t come from nowhere — it’s the resolution of a false dichotomy that has persisted since the early days of relativistic physics. The standard block universe is static and compatible with relativity, but it cannot account for the passage of time or quantum collapse. The growing block universe captures temporal flow (an expanding “now”), but it clashes with relativity because it privileges a universal present moment. Neither works alone.

Aethic reasoning’s insight: the two models aren’t competitors — they’re complementary halves of a richer structure. The block universe contributes its static, relativistic consistency; the growing block contributes its temporal dynamism. What’s needed is an extra degree of freedom — orthogonal to all four spacetime dimensions — along which the “growth” occurs without breaking the block structure. This is the Aethic dimension. It’s not an excuse-driven dimension (like those in string theory); it’s arrived at by directed effort to reconcile the two models’ failures through one extra degree of freedom.

The Coin Flip Epiphany (August 2022)

The extrusion principle epiphany occurred in a single instant while Ajax walked across his college campus and imagined flipping a penny. The realization: the web of all possible outcomes isn’t distributed across external parallel timelines — it’s compressed into the Aethic superposition content of a single block universe. The priority of order reverses: a block universe encompasses a multiverse (as superposition content), rather than a multiverse encompassing portions of a block universe. Space is fixed; the Aethic degrees of freedom vary.

How This Differs from Many-Worlds

Many-Worlds is an extension of the growing block universe: all deviations branch at the present, external to the existing block. The Aethic model has no intrinsic dependence on the present moment for branching — all possibilities are already encoded as superpositions within each block universe. The distinction is sharpest under time-reversal: Many-Worlds, like Copenhagen, says reversing time would un-collapse the superposition. The Aethic interpretation says this is impossible — in the subsequent block universe, there was never a superposition to un-collapse.

Using Schrödinger’s cat: in the prior block universe, the cat never leaves superposition — even at 2:30 PM when the experimenter checks, the prior block universe still has the cat in superposition (with the experimenter now entangled). But the experimenter’s consciousness cannot be located there (the cat being dead is a perceivable difference), so they extrude to the subsequent block universe where the cat was always alive (or always dead). The Aethic Base Principle of Superpositions (Principle 3) states: an agent’s consciousness occupies whichever block universe maximizes surrounding superposition content without alerting them to a discernible difference between any two states.

Wigner’s Friend & the Qualitative Facet

A natural objection: if Wigner and his friend sit across a table from each other, and the cat’s fate is revealed behind Wigner so only his friend sees it, how can they be at different Aethic locations while staring at each other in the same room?

The Aethic answer: “the same physical space” is an oversimplification. We already know from Zeno’s paradox that disregarding derivatives in a physical space leads to paradoxes, and from special relativity that disregarding time as a dimensional component leads to paradoxes. The Aethic dimension is qualitative rather than spatial — even as Wigner stares at his friend, his friend is “qualitatively infused” with their own unique Aethus. The degrees of freedom distinguishing Wigner’s view of the room from his friend’s are Aethic degrees of freedom, and they are every bit as real as spatial ones. This concept is underdeveloped in Western philosophy but has been studied in the East for thousands of years — the closest English word for this qualitative infusion is “consciousness,” though the paper argues it’s something more fundamental.

The Tricky Principle — Why This Ontology Is Forced

The deeper justification runs through the tricky principle (Principle 14): we cannot know whether reality outside one’s observation follows the same rules as within it. Before quantum mechanics, this was unfalsifiable — Occam’s razor demanded consistency (classical metaphysics). But quantum mechanics empirically demonstrates that a single unobserved object follows different rules, so Occam’s razor flips: whatever lies outside observation must follow different metaphysical rules.

The extrusion principle is the specific proposal for what those rules look like. Its correctness is constrained by the requirement that it reproduce all quantum phenomena when specialized to the quantum scale. The procedure: record how phenomena work behind the tricky principle using the quantum world as an aid, then generalize to all scales by induction. The right generalization preserves relations rather than bluntly scaling up the Born rule (just as the round Earth generalizes correctly from the locally flat surface, rather than scaling up the flatness itself).

Retrocausality Dissolved & the Aethic Butterfly Effect

The extrusion-consequential principle (Principle 12) establishes that the indiscriminate time principle requires the extrusion principle. Under timeline-branching, past events solidify as time passes them — changing a past event requires retrocausality, contradicting the timeline’s structure. The extrusion principle replaces timelines with block universes: there’s no arrow that solidifies events, only an Aethic transition to a different block universe whose history was always consistent.

The bank robbery illustrates a striking consequence. You witness a robbery; the manager is shot. You time-travel back 5 minutes. Classically, the same robbers arrive. Aethically, the identity of the robbers, the manager, and the bystanders are all re-rolled — anyone whose identity you hadn’t Aethically locked in. The Aethic butterfly effect is continuous rather than dichotomous: unlike the classical version (predate someone’s birth = totally different, don’t = totally the same), the Aethic version has divergences accumulate little by little as you predate each moment you learned each fact, however scattered in chronological time.

The First Postulate & Philosophical Framework
The observer as reference frame
+

From the extrusion principle, it follows that the Aethus must be fundamentally subjective — tied to a particular observer’s informational state. Wigner and his friend, who have different knowledge about Schrödinger’s cat, occupy different Aethae. This is not anti-realism or solipsism; the Aethus is an “informational analogue to a reference frame” in special relativity.

First Postulate of the Aethus Any attribution of realism is a statement of relation to a particular Aethus.

The Kevin the Rabbit thought experiment establishes why Aethic truth is a third epistemological category. Kevin hears a crash in the bushes and processes a high probability of death, P(D). A bird overhead assigns a different P(D) — ruling out Platonic objectivism. But P(D) is independent of Kevin’s opinions or will — ruling out Protagorean relativism. Aethic truth is what remains: perspectival-realist, observer-indexed but structurally constrained, aligned with the Sanskrit concept of Jñāna.

The Emily and Sam thought experiment develops the consequences. Emily meets Sam abroad and discovers he grew up next door. As 8-year-old Emily stares at that house, Sam’s family is in disagreeing superposition with every other possible occupant — a “bustling Aethic marketplace” of possible residents. Even Emily’s father, who may know who lives there, has the contents of his mind in superposition relative to Emily’s Aethus until she asks him.

The reversal principle inverts classical metaphysics: rather than building reality from exhaustively specified “possible worlds,” start from the Aethus (partial information) and generate reality from there. Your friend’s car in the parking lot is in disagreeing superposition over every spot when your back is turned. Turning around performs centric unfolding, transiting you to a new Aethus. The jarring consequence: every time you imagine what’s happening elsewhere, you are picturing something that is not your reality. Your actual reality is the raw superposition itself.

A rabbit named Kevin is sitting in a field. A hawk is circling overhead. What are Kevin’s chances of being eaten today?

Here’s the thing: Kevin’s chances don’t depend on what Kevin believes. A confident rabbit and a terrified rabbit have the same probability of being caught. The hawk doesn’t care about Kevin’s opinions. So probability isn’t about beliefs. It’s about the actual situation Kevin is in.

But here’s the twist: it also doesn’t depend on some God’s-eye view of the universe. There is no camera floating above everything that records “the real truth.” There’s just Kevin, in his field, with his information. And there’s the hawk, with its information. And these two perspectives might give genuinely different answers about what’s real, not because one is wrong, but because reality is perspective-dependent.

There is no view from nowhere. This is the First Postulate. Reality is always indexed to someone’s information, the way a speed is always relative to something. Asking “what’s really happening, independent of anyone’s perspective?” is like asking “how fast am I going, independent of anything else?” The question doesn’t have an answer. Not because we’re ignorant, but because that’s how reality works.

This might sound like it’s saying truth is just opinion. It’s not. Your beliefs don’t change reality. Kevin can’t think his way out of being eaten. But reality is anchored to a perspective. It’s not subjective (your wishes don’t matter) and it’s not objective (there’s no God’s eye view). It’s a third thing: perspectival. Structurally constrained, but always indexed to an observer.

This is surprisingly close to ideas that have existed for thousands of years in Eastern philosophy. In Buddhism, the concept of Śūnyatā says that phenomena lack inherent, standalone existence; things are real only in relation to other things. In Hinduism, the Advaita Vedanta tradition holds that the distinctions we draw between self and world are not fundamental. These traditions arrived at structurally similar conclusions through very different methods. The framework argues this convergence isn’t a coincidence. It’s evidence that the underlying structure is real.

The takeaway: Reality isn’t a story told from no one’s perspective. It’s always indexed to an observer’s informational state, the way velocity is always relative to a reference frame. This is the First Postulate — and it means the question “what’s really happening?” always needs an answer to “relative to whom?” first.

The Gettier Problem — Unsolvable by Design

In the stopped-clock case (Alice looks at a broken clock that coincidentally shows the correct time), the Aethic diagnosis is that assessing Alice’s “knowledge” requires referencing an Aethus independent of hers, one that knows the clock is broken. But by the first postulate, that external Aethus’s reality is not intrinsically relevant to Alice’s. The Gettier problem is declared unsolvable by design, akin to Gödel’s incompleteness theorem, but for epistemological assessment, because no mechanism within a single Aethus can distinguish lucky true belief from knowledge without importing external information.

Dual Logic: Abstract vs. Causal

Aethic reasoning distinguishes abstract logic (structural rules like De Morgan’s theorem, invariant across all Aethae) from causal logic (empirical relationships that vary with one’s Aethus). Mathematical identities fall under the attribute cascade as derivable statements — they’re always present. The classical conflation of these two domains is identified as a key philosophical barrier to solving the measurement problem.

Even the truth value of Aethic reasoning itself cannot be placed in Aethic superposition, because that would create a self-referential paradox (an invalid Aethus). This is why Aethic reasoning is classified as abstract logic rather than causal logic — its validity is structural, not empirical.

The Ontological Reversal

Since the Aethus is epistemologically more fundamental than a possible world, it is also ontologically more fundamental (by Occam’s razor — otherwise ontology would rely on an arbitrary epistemological system). The notion of “the real possible world” dissolves; all Aethae are equally real with respect to themselves. Realism becomes a two-node system: “what is the object” against “what is the reality” — and the reality node is an Aethus, not a fixed possible world.

The Forbidden-Epistemic Antirealism Principle

The paper directly confronts the common philosophical stance that quantum mechanics is fundamentally beyond rational comprehension. This comes in two flavors: (1) the human brain evolved in classical settings and simply lacks the scope for quantum ontology, and (2) the quantum world itself is an ontological black box. The paper calls both quantum epistemic antirealism and declares them paradoxical.

Principle 17 — Forbidden-Epistemic Antirealism The extrapolation from quantum uncertainty to epistemic antirealism confuses an uncertainty principle over causal logic with one over abstract logic. Arguing that reality cannot be rationally comprehended is itself a statement in abstract logic — and therefore paradoxical. The very notion of a measurement problem solution can be contrasted with epistemic antirealism.

The historical parallel is striking: this is exactly how humanity approached the motion of planets before Newton — invisible angels pulling them along their paths. The “mysticism” was simply a psychological phenomenon following from the lack of a clear axiomatic framework of gravity. Jaynes’s famous quote about the “scrambled omelette” of quantum mechanics captures the modern equivalent. The paper argues we are living in the time prior to the epistemic revolution, just as pre-Newtonian thinkers were — and we know from Newton’s success that such a revolution is possible.

The paper further distinguishes first-order epistemic antirealism (each new discovery is more impersonal than the last, as if by ontological trajectory) from second-order epistemic antirealism (the quantum world is categorically beyond any rational framework). The first is directly contested by the extrusion principle bringing quantum effects into one’s personal backyard. The second is contested by the dual logic principle — it confuses the category of the barrier.

Simulation, Dreams & Existential Significance

The simulation hypothesis loses its bite under Aethic reasoning. If you don’t know which level of a simulation stack you’re in, the second postulate puts you in superposition across all levels — perhaps even an agreeing one if some kind of uncertainty principle applies. And even discovering you are in a simulation changes nothing existentially: by the first postulate, your simulated Aethus is no less real than any other Aethus. No Aethus is existentially superior to any other — that’s simply not how the Aethic tree works.

The “pavement but really grass” dream-world (from the Accidentalism principle, Layer 9) extends this: even if every time you walk on pavement you’re actually walking on grass, the Aethus asserts that this qualia now lines up with whatever qualia you involuntarily aligned it with before. The mediocrity principle argues it was probably the same yesterday too. The Aethic postulates still apply regardless of simulation depth, because of their relational nature.

The Hard Problem of Consciousness

The paper approaches the hard problem through a dualism principle:

Principle 36 — Aethus-Reality Dualism Just as for any reality there must be a corresponding Aethus (first postulate), for any valid Aethus there is automatically a corresponding reality. This is the converse of the first postulate — both directions hold.

Biological life, by climbing the evolutionary ladder toward more sophisticated information-gathering mechanisms, implies a corresponding Aethus arising in parallel. Even a flatworm gauging water salinity has the grounds for a rudimentary Aethus — its neurons encode a neurological assessment that functions as ghost degrees of freedom. The paper poses the central question: is that flatworm’s Aethus its Aethus in the same way as yours is yours? If the structure is the same, consciousness becomes a matter of degree rather than kind — emerging naturally from the Aethic architecture rather than requiring a separate explanation.
