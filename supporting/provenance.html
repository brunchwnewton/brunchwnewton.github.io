Provenance Record

Aethic Reasoning — a one-page evidentiary index of the framework's origin, development, and the scope of AI involvement. Every claim below links to a dated, independently hosted record. Last updated July 2026.

This page exists to answer one question completely and briefly: who originated this framework, and what role did AI play? The short answer: the framework's conceptual core — its three postulates, its central invalidation argument, and its re-indexing semantics — was developed and published by the author alone in May 2023, before any AI collaboration; development continued solo through a public, institutionally timestamped version record from 2024 to 2026; AI assistance (Claude, Anthropic) began in early 2026 — first Claude Opus 4.6, later Claude Fable 5 — and was confined to formalization, algorithmic streamlining of the author's own deductions, prose drafting under direction, and literature interface, with the direction of authority documented inside the papers themselves, timestamped to the minute. The records below let a skeptical reader verify each clause of that sentence in minutes.

Beyond the Aethic papers

The author's separate paper on sequential achievement was, by contrast, heavily AI-assisted in its formal construction — and is documented under the same discipline. The concept (an exponential-to-linear transition in the time complexity of multi-step intellectual work) was first recorded in the author's journal on Sep 25, 2024, 2:16 PM; the first quantitative attempt (a quantile-based interpolation) on Sep 30, 2024, 2:56 PM — by the author's own account, crude relative to the final form; the idea then developed across months of journal entries. The formalization was a single documented collaboration (Claude Opus 4.6) to which the author brought a completed graph of constraints and logical dependencies with deliberately blank edges, and the model's role was to fill those edges with the standard statistical machinery. The record shows the division concretely. The author specifies the interface and the model must find the formula: "I don't know but want to know the mean and standard deviation of A… What I DO know, though, is the mode of B, what the quantile of the mode of B is in A, and a single data point for the highest performer" (msg 3). The author audits the model's output empirically and interrogates the mathematics rather than accepting it: "when I ran this, I got z_q about 4.2 and z_max at roughly 6.3… why does that consistency constraint between them have to hold so fiercely?" (msg 17). The author overrides on conceptual grounds — challenging the normality assumption with a Pareto intuition — and then issues the acceptance verdict on the model's proposed resolution in ontological, not formal, terms: "We keep the normal distribution, physically reinterpret the parameter itself… AND we sort of get an emergent Pareto picture" (msgs 19–21). And the author sets the model's parameters from his own data where the model has none: "I actually think Einstein SHOULD have crossed into the linear regime… we have to tweak the n number of steps"; "n = ~60 is what it took me, so let's go with that" (msgs 77–79). Each quotation is verifiable against the sealed record by its per-message hash. The full chat record of that collaboration has been recovered from the official account export and preserved under a hash-sealed archive: a single conversation (March 13–26, 2026), 322 messages in near-even alternation (160 human, 162 model), reconstructed by parent-pointer traversal and sealed with a SHA-256 integrity chain — a hash of the source export file, a hash of the reconstructed thread, and a hash of every individual message, with the fingerprints independently timestamped. The record shows the dense correction-and-verdict pattern the account above describes: the author repeatedly overriding the model's formal choices on conceptual grounds, the model supplying the statistical machinery under those verdicts. Selected excerpts — the author's constraint and correction messages, verbatim, each carrying its per-message hash — will be published here; the complete record is held privately and is available to legitimate verifiers under confidentiality, where the per-message hashes permit exact verification of every excerpt against it.

Two questions, answered plainly

Did an AI make this discovery?
No. The framework's core was published in May 2023, predating the collaboration by three years and predating any model capable of contributing to work of this kind. The dates are third-party.
Did an AI write parts of the 2026 papers?
Yes — and the papers say so, in detail, with timestamps, including what the model got wrong. That disclosure is the point: the collaboration is documented, not discovered. The scope was formalization and drafting under direction; the constraints, the verdicts, and the framework were the author's.
